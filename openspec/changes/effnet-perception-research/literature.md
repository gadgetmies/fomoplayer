# Literature review: what Discogs-EffNet (multi) treats as similar

Scope: what published evidence says `discogs_multi_embeddings-effnet-bs64-1` should be invariant to, or should encode, under our perturbations. Claims were checked against the cited sources. Our own extrapolations are marked **(inference)**.

## 1. How Discogs-EffNet was trained

- **Backbone and input.** EfficientNet-B0 on 2-second patches of 96-band mel-spectrograms, computed with the MusiCNN front-end in Essentia. Input shape is 64×128×96 (batch × frames × bands) at 16 kHz mono. The 1280-d embedding is the flattened last conv block, with a 512-d projection head on top. Training data was Discogs-4M (in-house; 3.3M tracks used). ([Alonso-Jiménez, Serra, Bogdanov, ISMIR 2022](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.7316790); [model JSON](https://essentia.upf.edu/models/feature-extractors/discogs-effnet/discogs_multi_embeddings-effnet-bs64-1.json)). Essentia source sets frame 512, 96 bands and log10 compression ([tensorflowinputmusicnn.cpp](https://github.com/MTG/essentia/blob/master/src/algorithms/spectral/tensorflowinputmusicnn.cpp)). The default patch hop is 62 frames, which gives about 1 embedding/s ([TensorflowPredictEffnetDiscogs](https://essentia.upf.edu/reference/std_TensorflowPredictEffnetDiscogs.html)).
- **Objective.** COLA-style contrastive learning with bilinear similarity. Other pairs in the batch act as negatives. The paper notes COLA was chosen partly for "requiring no data augmentation": positives are random 2 s crops, with no pitch, tempo or EQ augmentation described ([ISMIR 2022](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.7316790)).
- **Positive pairs per association.** *Track*: two crops of the same track. *Release*: shared release. *Artist*: shared artist, different release. *Label*: shared label, no shared artist. The multi-task model uses all four heads with a shared encoder. Loss weights are track 0.1, release 0.15, artist 0.6, label 0.15, and the encoder was initialised from the artist model. The authors report it "generally … close in performance to the Artist model" (same source).
- **Doc discrepancy (uncertain).** The [Essentia models page](https://essentia.upf.edu/models.html) describes the multi model as "artist and track associations". The paper and the model JSON ("artist, label, release, and track similarities") describe four. We treat the paper as authoritative.
- **Related, not this model:** playlist pairs beat artist pairs for similarity ([ICASSP 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12257)), and Discogs-trained MAEST benefits from longer inputs ([ISMIR 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16418)).

## 2. Robustness and invariance of music embeddings

- **Contrastive training without augmentation keeps key and tempo in the embedding.** For models trained without time-stretch (TS) or pitch-shift (PS) augmentation, the cosine distance between track-average embeddings shows "troughs … at half and double time" for stretched audio. For shifted audio it shows troughs at 4th/5th/octave and peaks at minor 2nd and tritone. TS/PS augmentation removes these effects, and nearest neighbours become tempo/key-biased without it ([McCallum et al., ICASSP 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.08889)).
- **Tagging CNNs on clean data** (deformations applied only at test time, via MUDA): ±1 semitone, time stretch by 2^±½, dynamic range compression and white noise. DRC was "the least influential" and heavy noise "the most critical". MusiCNN, which uses the same front-end as Discogs-EffNet, was robust to time-stretch but "relatively vulnerable against pitch shift" ([Won et al., SMC 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.00751)).
- **Retrieval with CLMR/CLAP.** Retrieval holds within ±3 semitones and breaks beyond about ±10% stretch. White noise was tolerated only at very low levels ([Barnett et al., 2024, preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.14542)).
- **Codecs.** MFCCs are stable above about 160 kbps, and chroma is virtually unaffected by codec or bitrate ([Urbano et al., ISMIR 2014](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2014/paper/000326.pdf)). Systematic degradation tooling (MP3, filters, noise, IRs, speed-up) comes from the Audio Degradation Toolbox ([Mauch & Ewert, ISMIR 2013](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2013/paper/000145.pdf)). We found no codec study of Discogs-EffNet itself.
- **Effects as embedding trajectories.** Gain, low-pass, reverb and bitcrush move OpenL3/PANNs/CLAP embeddings monotonically along one direction, but within a high-dimensional subspace. Projecting that direction out does not restore robustness ([Deng et al., ICASSP 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.15900)).
- **Key information in tagging CNNs is weak.** On MARBLE GiantSteps key, MusiCNN scores 14.4 against about 62–67 for MERT ([Yuan et al., NeurIPS 2023 D&B](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10548)). Broader benchmarks: [HEAR (Turian et al., 2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.03022), [SynTheory probing (Wei et al., ISMIR 2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00872).

## 3. Stems and temporal pooling

- **Stem-level perception differs from mix-level similarity.** In ABX tests on Slakh2100 stems, perceived similarity depended on which part listeners attended to, and rhythm and melody mattered more than timbre. Existing similarity features mostly captured timbre ([Hashizume & Toda, ICASSP 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02138)). Separated stems as input reduce similarity accuracy "owing to artifacts" ([Hashizume et al., 2025 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17281)).
- **Gap.** We found no published stem-ablation study of Discogs-EffNet or similar tagging/metadata embeddings that shows which source dominates. Our stem experiments appear novel.
- **Pooling.** Combining pooling functions (mean+max, mean+var) beat single mean pooling for tagging. Variance helps only when combined with mean ([Hamel, Lemieux, Bengio & Eck, ISMIR 2011](https://ismir2011.ismir.net/papers/PS6-13.pdf)). McCallum et al. measure robustness on *track-average* embeddings, so mean pooling is a standard baseline.

## 4. Time-stretch and pitch-shift artefacts

- **WSOLA** produces "transient doubling or stuttering" and transient skipping. **Phase vocoder** TSM smears transients and adds "phasiness", described as "reverberant", with "less bite". HPSS hybrids combine PV for harmonic content with OLA for percussive content ([Driedger & Müller, Applied Sciences 2016](https://doi.org/10.3390/app6020057)).
- **Pitch shift = resample + TSM,** so TSM artefacts carry over. Upward shifts move formants (the "chipmunk effect") unless the spectral envelope is corrected (same source).
- **Rubber Band.** R2 is a phase vocoder with phase reset at percussive transients. R3 "produces higher-quality results … especially complex mixes … and music with substantial bass content" ([technical notes](https://breakfastquay.com/rubberband/technical.html), [README](https://github.com/breakfastquay/rubberband)). MUDA uses it through pyrubberband ([McFee et al., ISMIR 2015](https://github.com/bmcfee/muda)).

## Implications for our measurements

- **Same-track crops are positives.** The track head (and the release/artist heads) therefore pull together crops from different parts of a track. We expect relative invariance to bar shuffling and section choice, and good behaviour from mean pooling. Reversal changes the frames themselves, so it is not covered **(inference)**.
- **No augmentation was used in training,** so expect tempo and key to be encoded. Time-stretch and pitch shift should move embeddings a lot, possibly with half/double-time and 5th/octave troughs (per McCallum et al.). Varispeed changes both at once and should move them most **(inference)**. BPM/key correlations with distance are likely real, not noise.
- **Artist weight dominates (0.6),** so the embedding should track production and timbre identity (artist/label "sound") more than musical content. Label correlations may be strong for D&B labels with house sounds **(inference)**.
- **Codec and EQ.** The input is 16 kHz, so spectrum above 8 kHz is discarded. High-bitrate MP3/AAC and high-pass/low-pass cutoffs above 8 kHz should be near-invariant. Low-pass below 8 kHz, bass EQ and heavy noise should not be **(inference; no direct EffNet codec study found)**.
- **Separate algorithm artefacts from tempo effects.** Run TS/PS with at least two engines (Rubber Band R2 and R3, or PV and WSOLA). D&B breaks are transient-dense, so smearing or doubling can confound "tempo" sensitivity.
- **Stem results are new territory.** Interpret drums/bass dominance cautiously: Demucs artefacts are themselves a perturbation, so compare against an "unmix-remix all stems" control.
